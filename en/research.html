Research

I study how discourses and imaginaries around socio-environmental issues are constructed, communicated and legitimised, drawing on media studies, political ecology, ecofeminism and Latin American decolonial thought.

Future research

Ecosocial imaginaries, participation and media culture

This proposal examines how ecosocial imaginaries are narratively constructed, communicated and contested within contemporary media culture. Drawing on Beckert's theory of fictional expectations, critical discourse analysis and Latin American communication traditions, it asks what future visions ecosocial narratives propose, how they circulate, what communicative strategies they employ, and under what social, cultural and epistemic conditions they do (or do not) gain credibility.

The corpus includes speculative fiction, audiovisual media, political manifestos, social media content and theoretical literature. Building on my prior work on ecosocial podcasting and climate communication, it seeks to understand how future imaginaries navigate epistemic hierarchies and challenge the colonisation of the imagination by dominant narratives.
