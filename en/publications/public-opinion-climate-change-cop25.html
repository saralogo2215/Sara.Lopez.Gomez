Public Opinion about Climate Change in United States, Partisan View and Media Coverage of the 2019 United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP 25) in Madrid

Castillo Esparcia, A., & López-Gómez, S.
Sustainability, 13(7), 3926

Abstract

The research examines news on climate change in different media, through the analysis of agenda setting and framing, in the context of the construction of media discourse. The media have played a relevant role in the symbolic struggle over images of climate change. The polarised public opinion on climate change in the USA—which led the Trump administration to withdraw from the Paris Agreement and revoke environmental policies—is analysed through the coverage that media with Republican and Democratic leanings gave to the climate crisis during the 12 days of the 2019 Climate Summit. A total of 189 news articles published by Fox News, Breitbart, CNN and the New York Times were identified, analysed and compared. The results reveal that media with a Republican leaning were the only ones to publish climate change denial news. Breitbart published the largest number of items in the sample, 71% of them denialist, using tactics such as the spectacularisation of the climate phenomenon, ad hominem attacks on environmentalists and politicians, linking environmental initiatives with “eco-fascism” or the “radical left”, half-truth fallacies and questionable sources associated with the fossil fuel industry. Fox News barely covered the issue during the summit. Democratic-leaning media published no denial news; their information and opinion pieces conveyed environmental initiatives and the consequences of climate change.
